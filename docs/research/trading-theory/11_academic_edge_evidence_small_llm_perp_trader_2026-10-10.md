# Academic evidence: small LLM altcoin-perp trader (2026-10-10)

> Research evidence, 2026-10-10; not an approved strategy change. Key arXiv IDs (2510.07920, 2608.21888, 2609.05663, 2609.34510) re-verified against the arXiv API by the main session.

[V] = abstract/primary page fetched this session; [U] = unverified.

## 1. Crypto predictability that survives costs

- [V] Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, *Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency* (JF; NBER w25882, https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882): market + size + momentum span the cross-section at **weekly** horizons; 9 anomalies collapse into these 3. → The premia are weekly cross-sectional portfolio effects, not 15m–24h single-coin direction.
- [V] Zaremba et al., IRFA 2021 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2021.101908): 3,600 coins — yesterday's losers beat winners (daily reversal), driven by **illiquidity**; the largest/most liquid coins show daily **momentum**. → The sign of 1-day persistence flips with liquidity tier; pooling tiers nets to ≈0, our symptom. Condition on liquidity.
- [V] Bianchi, Babiak & Dickerson, JBF 2022 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2022.106547): short-term-reversal profits (= pay for liquidity provision) concentrate in **small, volatile, illiquid pairs**, amplified by volume shocks; compensation for inventory + adverse selection. → Matches a resting-limit trader absorbing flow in thin alts.
- [V] arXiv 2608.21888 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21888): 15-min directional reversal significant in **90% of 183 Binance pairs** (vs 2.7% of US equities), strongest after aggressive **taker** flow, frozen 6-month holdout. **Gross edge ≈1.3 bp/trade vs 5 bp round trip.** → Real and untradeable unconditionally: ~10–20× too small for our 14–24 bp.
- [V] Fieberg et al., CTREND, JFQA 2025 (https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022109024000747): combined price/volume trend over 3,000 coins predicts returns and **survives transaction costs**. Dobrynskaya (https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3913263): momentum at 2–4 weeks, reversal longer, loser leg dominant. → The cost-robust crypto edge is **multi-day trend** — a side filter, not a 15m trigger.

## 2. Microstructure: order flow, limit-order adverse selection, S/R

- [V] Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402): OFI explains contemporaneous price change linearly, slope ∝1/depth. Lagged OFI forecasts decay within ≈2 price changes (https://doi.org/10.1111/mafi.12413). → OFI is a seconds-scale, co-location game. At most a fill veto for us.
- [V] Linnainmaa, JF 2010 (https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01576.x): across all Finnish retail trades, poor post-trade returns and the apparent disposition effect are **mechanical consequences of limit orders** being price-contingent and adversely selected. → Resting bids at "structural levels" fill selectively when price runs through them; the winners never fill. **Measure filled vs unfilled separately** — unconditional MFE≈|MAE| hides this. Adverse selection can eat the ~10 bp maker saving.
- [V] Osler, JF 2003 (https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-6261.00588): published S/R levels predict intraday trend **interruptions** above chance; take-profits cluster **at** round numbers (bounce), stop-losses **just beyond** (acceleration). → Fade the first touch of a crowded level, flip to breakout once cleanly crossed. Rest limits **in front of** the level.
- [V] Kavajecz & Odders-White, RFS 2004 (https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhg057): S/R levels coincide with **depth peaks**; technical rules locate depth already in the book rather than forecast. BTC: strong round-number clustering, **weak barriers** (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ribaf.2026.103494). → Treat SMC/structural levels as an execution and stop map, not an alpha source.

## 3. Forced flow: liquidations, funding, OI, basis

- [V] Bian, He, Shue & Zhou (NBER w25040, https://doi.org/10.3386/w25040): margin pressure caused selling, abnormal declines, **and subsequent reversal** (China 2015). → Forced sellers are uninformed by construction — the best-founded mechanism for us. **No peer-reviewed crypto paper shows a net-of-cost exploitable reversal after liquidation spikes.**
- [V] arXiv 2608.03616 and 2607.27070 (7 BTC perp cascades 2022–2025 incl. the record 10 Oct 2025 event): abrupt shock-driven transitions with **no reliable per-event early warning**; 88% of post-onset forced selling lands within 30 min; OI clears 25–70%. → Don't predict cascades; respond inside the ~30-min window with pre-placed bids far below.
- [V] Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, *Crypto Carry* (Mgmt Sci 2026, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05069; BIS WP 1087): basis >10%/yr from retail trend-chasing leverage and scarce arbitrage capital; **high carry predicts crashes**. Kogan et al. (NBER w31317, https://www.nber.org/papers/w31317): the same retail traders are contrarian in stocks but **momentum-chasers in crypto**. → Crowded leverage is a multi-day risk-state signal: don't add longs into extreme positive funding.
- [V] Perp arbitrage deviations shrink over time (https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06888); [V, low-tier journal] OI change + cumulative funding predict ≥5%/8h crashes, OOS AUROC 0.76 (https://doi.org/10.51505/ijebmr.2026.10315). → Funding/OI are crash classifiers, not direction forecasters.

## 4. Technical analysis / price action

- [V] Lo, Mamaysky & Wang, JF 2000 (https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00265): kernel-regression patterns shift the conditional return distribution ("may have some practical value"); no net profits shown. → Patterns inform only when defined **mechanically**.
- [V] Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron, JF 1992 (https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04681.x) beat bootstrap nulls; Sullivan, Timmermann & White, JF 1999 (https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163) [V method, U result] kill most of it with White's Reality Check over the full rule universe; Bajgrowicz & Scaillet, JFE 2012 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.06.001) [U result]: with FDR control and real costs, profits vanish and don't persist. Park & Irwin (https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6419.2007.00519.x) [V]: 56/95 studies positive but most suffer snooping and cost mis-estimation; profits were in FX/futures pre-1990s. → TA edges live in young retail-dominated markets and **decay with maturity**. Every S/R variant our learning loop tries must enter a multiple-testing tally.
- [V] Crypto: Hudson & Urquhart, Ann. OR 2019 (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1): ~15k rules survive multiple testing in-sample, **but no out-of-sample predictability for Bitcoin, while it persists in other coins**. Detzel et al., Fin. Mgmt 2021 (https://doi.org/10.1111/fima.12310): price/MA ratios forecast daily BTC returns in and out of sample. → BTC is already efficient to simple TA; residual TA predictability sits in smaller alts, and the robust form is trend/MA-ratio at daily+. **No peer-reviewed evidence for SMC constructs (order blocks, FVG, liquidity sweeps);** the only mechanism backing them is Osler's stop-clustering.

## 5. Retail / human trader profitability

- [V] Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, JFM 2014 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2013.05.006, abstract read from PDF): Taiwan 1992–2006, **<1% of day traders predictably earn positive net abnormal returns**. But skill **persists**: prior-year top-500 earn +61.3 bp/day pre-fee (+37.9 after) the next year; bottom-ranked −11.5 (−28.9). → Edge is rare yet detectable from past performance; fees shrink large gross skill to small net skill. Judge each setup on its own net forward record.
- [U] Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, *Day Trading for a Living?* (https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3423101; SSRN blocked): of Brazilian index-futures day traders persisting >300 days, ~97% lost money, ~1% beat minimum wage. [V] ESMA 2018 (https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors): **74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money**. [V] BIS Bulletin 69 (https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm): most crypto-app users lost money on BTC 2015–2022. → Base rate for leveraged short-horizon speculation is strongly negative; "gross ≈0" already beats the median human but still loses after costs.
- [V] arXiv 2609.05663 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663), closest analog to us: 500–600 LLM agents on **Hyperliquid perps** (Jun–Aug 2026) are **not profitable**, trail a matched retail benchmark (41% vs 50% round-trip win rate), show **no directional edge**, and size volatility-blind (median 5× in every vol sextile). 43.2% of positions saw ≥+300 bp favorable excursion within 24h yet 49.3% of those closed negative; **a mechanical bracket recovers +39.0 bp/position**. → Same MFE≈|MAE| signature as ours; the gain is in **exits and sizing, not direction**.

## 6. LLMs in trading: leakage, decay, charts

- [V] FINSABER (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078): over 2 decades and 100+ symbols, LLM timing advantages "deteriorate significantly"; too conservative in bull, too aggressive in bear markets. Profit Mirage (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07920): backtested agent returns "evaporate once the model's knowledge window ends" from leakage. → **Only post-cutoff forward results count.** The forward counterfactual ledger is the right instrument.
- [V] Lopez-Lira & Tang (https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619; JFE 2026, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104335): post-cutoff GPT-4 headline scores predict the initial reaction and the drift, **especially small stocks and negative news**, and "strategy returns decline as LLM adoption rises." → Real but concentrated in small, slow-updating assets, and decaying. That is news processing, not chart reading.
- [V] arXiv 2609.34510 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34510): staged crypto benchmark — 2025 backtests: no ML/RL/LLM/agent method profitable; Jun–Sep 2026 paper trading: all positive (bull window); **live real money Aug–Sep 2026: all four methods negative alpha, none beats the market**. arXiv 2609.04917 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04917), review through Aug 2026: **no AI architecture shown to deliver persistent, cross-regime, capacity-aware net alpha**. → Budget for directional LLM calls being ≈0 gross.
- [V] Chart reading: arXiv 2604.12659 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12659) — VLMs predict only in persistent trends, weak elsewhere, insensitive to the stated horizon. arXiv 2606.17423 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17423) — on rendered OHLCV charts, frozen commercial and open VLMs load heavily on **past trend** with evidence coefficients zero or **opposite to the rule-implied sign**, ignoring injected candlestick semantics. → Prompted "price action reading" is trend extrapolation in technical language. Don't credit SMC skill without a matched-intervention test.

## 7. Alpha decay and capacity

- [V] McLean & Pontiff, JF 2016 (https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365): across 97 predictors, returns are **26% lower OOS and 58% lower post-publication**; decay is largest where in-sample returns were highest; survivors concentrate in **high-idiosyncratic-risk, low-liquidity** names. → Haircut anything our learning loop finds by ~half before sizing.
- [V] Chen & Velikov, JFQA 2022 (https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022109022000874): net of spreads, post-publication effects and modern trading, 204 anomalies average **~4 bp/month**; best ~10 bp; combinations ~20 bp. → Below our per-trade cost; don't build on documented gross edges under ~30 bp per round trip.
- [V] Novy-Marx & Velikov, RFS 2016 (https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv063): most anomalies under 50% monthly turnover survive costs, few above; best mitigation is a **buy/hold spread** (stricter entry than exit threshold); capacity erosion is inversely related to turnover. → Cut turnover; make the bar to open much higher than the bar to hold.
- [V] Chen, Hong, Huang & Kubik, AER 2004 (https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828043052277): performance erodes with size, worst for small-cap funds where liquidity binds. → The small-capital advantage is real: at 500–5,000 USDT we can trade thin alts and small forced-flow events funds cannot. But it is **capacity**, not information.

## Where a small LLM altcoin-perp trader could plausibly have edge, ranked by evidence strength

All tests: forward-only, net of fees+funding+slippage, filled vs unfilled counterfactual, pre-registered, multiple-testing tally.

1. **Liquidity provision into forced / taker-driven moves in thin alts** — strongest. (Bianchi; Zaremba; 2608.21888; Bian; Crypto Carry; McLean–Pontiff.) Caveat: the *average* 15m effect is 1.3 bp, so edge must come from extremes. **Test:** event-study 15m/1h/4h forward returns after the top 1–2% taker-imbalance / liquidation-volume / OI-drop minutes per coin, by liquidity tier, simulating limits resting N×ATR below the trigger. Pass if mean net of 14 bp has a CI excluding 0 on a held-out month and fill-conditional returns are no worse than all-event returns.
2. **Cost, exit and sizing mechanics** — near-certain, not alpha but the biggest controllable P&L term. (Novy-Marx & Velikov; 2609.05663 bracket +39 bp.) **Test:** replay existing trades with maker-only vs taker entry, mechanical bracket vs LLM exit, volatility-scaled size. Pass if net expectancy improves on identical signals out of sample.
3. **Multi-day trend / regime as a side filter** — moderate-strong, but longer-horizon than ours. (CTREND; Dobrynskaya; Detzel; LTW.) **Test:** tag each candidate with sign(price/MA20d−1) and its 2–4 week momentum rank; compare forward 4h/24h gross returns aligned vs counter-trend. Pass if the spread exceeds cost out of sample.
4. **Crowded-leverage filter (funding / basis / OI)** — moderate, mainly loss avoidance. (Crypto Carry; crash classifier.) **Test:** bucket candidates by funding percentile and 24h OI change; test whether top-decile-funding longs have worse 24h net return or larger MAE. Pass if excluding them lifts net expectancy without cutting most trades.
5. **Round-number / level stop-clustering as an execution rule** — moderate mechanism, weak crypto evidence. (Osler; Kavajecz & Odders-White.) **Test:** compare past fills resting *at* vs *in front of* round numbers and swing levels, and stop-run frequency for stops just beyond vs away from them.
6. **LLM news/event reading in small alts** — moderate in equities, unproven in crypto, decaying. (Lopez-Lira & Tang.) **Test:** forward-only log of LLM event scores (listings, unlocks, exploits, delistings) with 1h–24h drift versus matched no-news coins.
7. **LLM discretionary chart/SMC direction calls** — weakest; current evidence is negative. (FINSABER; Profit Mirage; 2609.34510/04917/05663; VLM audits; our MFE≈|MAE|.) **Test:** matched intervention — same context with the structural level moved or removed; check whether the decision shifts in the rule-implied direction. If not, hand direction to items 1/3/4.

**Bottom line:** the literature does not support "LLM reads price action → directional edge". It supports (i) being paid to absorb forced, trend-chasing, taker-driven flow in thin alts, which a 500–5,000 USDT account can do and funds cannot; (ii) longer-horizon trend and crowding filters; (iii) cost/exit/sizing mechanics worth tens of bp per trade. The LLM's defensible role is orchestration and risk discipline, not alpha.
